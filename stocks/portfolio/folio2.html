Return — Two Ways to Measure It

XIRR · Money-Weighted

Accounts for when each dollar arrived — your deposits are staggered (biweekly/monthly), so this is the fair way to annualize a return built from irregular contributions. Computed from the actual cash-flow dates in your transaction history.
TWR · Time-Weighted
+46.28%
since inception (2024-08-14 → today) · IBKR "Performance / All"
Ignores the size/timing of deposits — measures pure investment skill/performance of the holdings themselves by chaining daily returns as if compounding a single dollar. Read directly off the IBKR dashboard's Performance toggle (the API tool only exposes trailing windows up to 1Y — trailing-1Y TWR is +3.67%, YTD 2026 is roughly flat at -0.03% — so the all-time +46.28% had to be confirmed manually).
Why XIRR (~9%) and TWR (+46.28%) look so different: this isn't a data error — it's what TWR vs. money-weighted return are built to show. TWR compounds the strategy's daily % moves independent of how much money was actually riding on them at the time; the small amount deployed right after the Aug 2024 launch caught early gains that then keep compounding in the TWR chain at full weight, even though most of your actual dollars only arrived much later (steady deposits through 2025–2026) and haven't had nearly as long to compound. XIRR is diluted by that — it weights the return by how much capital was in the account and for how long, so the late-arriving majority of your money pulls the annualized figure way down toward ~9%. Both are "correct": XIRR = "how well did my actual dollars do," TWR = "how well would $1 invested on day one have done, regardless of your contribution schedule."
